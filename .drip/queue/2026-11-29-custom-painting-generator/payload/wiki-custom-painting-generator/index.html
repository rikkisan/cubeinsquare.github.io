Custom paintings in Minecraft: how the painting generator builds them

Since 1.21 paintings are no longer a fixed list inside the game: a data pack can add new ones of any size up to 16 × 16 blocks, and a resource pack gives them a picture. The custom painting generator builds both packs from your images. This guide explains what is inside them, how to get the paintings onto a wall, and what usually goes wrong.

By Rikki

New paintings or replaced ones

There are two ways to put your own picture in a frame, and they suit different situations.

New paintingReplaced vanilla painting
Versions1.21 – 26.31.20 – 26.3

Replacing is the quick option for a single player world, or for a server you do not run, because a resource pack only changes what you see. A new painting suits a server or a map: it exists in the world for everyone and can have its own size, title and author.

What is in the two packs

For new paintings the generator writes a resource pack and a data pack with the same namespace (paintings unless you change it):

FileWhat it does
assets/paintings/textures/painting/<id>.pngThe picture. The game finds it through the painting's asset_id.
pack.pngThe icon in the resource pack list, cut from your first painting.
data/paintings/painting_variant/<id>.jsonRegisters the painting: its picture, size in blocks, title and author.
data/minecraft/tags/painting_variant/placeable.jsonLets the painting turn up when someone hangs a plain painting. Left out when no painting is marked for it.
pack.mcmeta (both packs)The pack format of your version: pack_format up to 1.21.8, min_format and max_format from 1.21.9.

A painting definition is short:

{
  "asset_id": "paintings:sunset_lake",
  "width": 3,
  "height": 2,
  "title": { "text": "Sunset lake", "color": "yellow" },
  "author": { "text": "Cube in Square", "color": "gray" }
}

asset_id points to assets/paintings/textures/painting/sunset_lake.png. width and height are in blocks, from 1 to 16. Title and author are text components for the tooltip; they exist from 1.21.2, so for 1.21 – 1.21.1 the generator leaves them out. Yellow and gray are the colors the vanilla paintings use.

Size, pixels and colors

The texture is the size in blocks times the pixels per block: a 3 × 2 painting at 16 pixels per block is a 48 × 32 picture. Vanilla paintings use 16, which is why a photo at that setting turns into chunky pixel art that looks at home next to them. The game stretches the texture over the whole painting, so 32, 64 or 128 pixels per block simply give a sharper picture. Beyond about 1024 pixels on a side the difference is hard to see, while the textures keep growing in players' graphics memory.

The color setting reduces the picture to 32, 16 or 8 colors taken from the image itself. With few colors, dithering mixes neighbouring pixels so a sky stays a gradient instead of breaking into bands; without it you get flat areas, like a poster. Contrast and saturation are applied before the colors are reduced. Paintings are drawn opaque, so transparent parts of a PNG are filled with the border color.

Crop to fill keeps the frame full and cuts the picture to the painting's shape; the two sliders move the cut. Whole image keeps everything and fills the rest with a border. Match the image shape changes the size in blocks to follow the picture, keeping the longer side.

What a big painting costs

For the server, size hardly matters. A painting is one entity whatever its size; every 5 seconds it checks that the blocks behind it are still solid and that nothing else hangs in its place. On a 26.3 server we hung 512 paintings of 16 × 16 blocks: they took 0.15 ms of the 50 ms each tick has, against 0.03 ms for 512 paintings of 1 × 1, and all 512 together added less than 80 KB to the world save.

The cost is on the players' side. The game puts the textures of all paintings into one image in video memory, at 4 bytes per pixel. A 16 × 16 painting at 128 pixels per block is 2048 × 2048 pixels, about 16 MB, and every player with the pack holds it. If that shared image grows beyond the largest texture the graphics card can hold, the game crashes with a Stitching error while it loads the pack: many older cards stop at 8192 pixels, most current ones at 16384. The generator adds this up in the Load line under the list of files and warns before you get there.

A big painting also needs a big wall: a flat area of solid blocks the size of the painting, with no item frame or other painting on it. Break one block behind it and it drops within 5 seconds.

Putting the painting on a wall

The give command depends on the version, and the generator writes the right one:

VersionCommand
1.21.5 – 26.3give @p minecraft:painting[minecraft:painting/variant="paintings:sunset_lake"]
1.20.5 – 1.21.4give @p minecraft:painting[minecraft:entity_data={id:"minecraft:painting",variant:"paintings:sunset_lake"}]
1.20 – 1.20.4give @p minecraft:painting{EntityTag:{variant:"minecraft:kebab"}} (vanilla paintings only)

From 1.21.5 the game checks the name: a typo gives Failed to get element instead of an item. Earlier versions accept any name, and a painting with an unknown name comes out as a vanilla one.

A plain painting from the crafting table works differently. When you hang it, the game takes every painting from the placeable list that fits the free wall, keeps only the largest of them and picks one of those at random. We read this in the game code of 26.3, and it means size decides. With Can appear when a plain painting is hung ticked, the generator adds yours to that list. A painting bigger than 4 × 4 then wins on every wall where it fits, because no vanilla painting is that big; a 2 × 2 one shares the draw with the eight vanilla 2 × 2 paintings and comes up only where nothing larger fits. Tick When a plain painting is hung, choose only from mine and the tag gets "replace": true: only your paintings are candidates. A wall where none of them fits then gets no painting at all, so keep at least one small painting in the set.

Installing

  1. Resource pack: into .minecraft/resourcepacks/, then Options → Resource Packs. On a server every player needs it, so offer it as the server resource pack (resource-pack in server.properties) or share the file.
  2. Data pack: into <world>/datapacks/, or add it on the data pack screen when you create a world.
  3. Leave the world and open it again, or restart the server.

The last step is the one people skip. Painting definitions are read only when the world loads. On 1.21.4 and 26.3 we added the pack to a running server and ran /reload: /datapack list showed it as enabled, but the paintings did not exist, and the placeable tag failed to load altogether because it named paintings the game did not know yet. After a restart everything worked.

Replacing vanilla paintings

A replacement only swaps the texture at assets/minecraft/textures/painting/<name>.png. The size stays the vanilla one, so pick a painting whose shape matches your picture; the generator lists the paintings your version has and suggests the closest shape.

What we checked

We loaded packs in this format on vanilla servers 1.20.4, 1.20.6, 1.21.1, 1.21.2, 1.21.4, 1.21.5, 1.21.11, 26.1, 26.2 and 26.3, and the generator's own downloads on 1.21.1, 1.21.4, 1.21.5 and 26.3:

  • On 1.20.x a painting_variant folder is ignored, and a painting asked for by a new name becomes kebab. That is why the generator only replaces paintings there.
  • From 1.21, paintings of 3 × 2, 2 × 2 and 16 × 16 blocks load, hang on a wall and stay there.
  • A painting 17 blocks wide stops the world from loading: the server logs Failed to load datapacks, can't proceed with server load and refuses every pack. The generator caps both sides at 16.
  • On a 26.3 server, 512 paintings of 16 × 16 blocks stayed on their walls and cost 0.15 ms per tick.
  • painting/variant works from 1.21.5; on 1.21.4 it is an unknown component. entity_data works on 1.20.5 – 1.21.4.
  • A placeable tag with "replace": true loads without errors.

How the picture looks on screen needs a player, so check the first painting in your own world before you hand the packs to a server.

FAQ

Do players on my server need the resource pack?

Yes. The data pack tells the server the painting exists; each player's game needs the picture. Without the resource pack they see purple and black squares where the painting hangs.

Do big paintings slow a server down?

No. A 16 × 16 painting is one entity, like a 1 × 1 one; in our test 512 of them cost 0.15 ms per tick. What grows with size is the texture players load and the wall the painting needs.

Can new paintings use the minecraft namespace?

The generator does not allow it: a new painting called minecraft:sunset would take the place of the vanilla one. Your own namespace keeps them apart and makes the pack easy to find and remove.

Can I change a painting after it is hung?

The picture, yes: build the resource pack again with the same ID and namespace, and every painting already on a wall shows the new picture. The size belongs to the data pack; after changing it, restart the world and hang the painting again.

Does it work on Bedrock?

No. Bedrock packs work differently and have no paintings defined by data packs; this generator is for Java Edition.
