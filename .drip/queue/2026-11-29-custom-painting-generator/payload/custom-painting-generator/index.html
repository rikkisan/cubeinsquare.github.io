Minecraft Custom Painting Generator

Turn your own pictures into paintings that hang on any wall. Pick a size from 1 × 1 to 16 × 16 blocks, crop the image, choose how many pixels and colors it keeps, and download packs for 1.20 to 26.3. Your images stay in the browser; nothing is uploaded.

Read the guide Paint a texture by hand

A new painting needs two downloads: a resource pack with the pictures and a data pack that adds them to the game. Replacing a vanilla painting needs only the resource pack.

New paintings need 1.21 or newer. On 1.20 – 1.20.6 the generator replaces vanilla paintings: pick one on each card below.

How to install

  1. Put the resource pack ZIP in .minecraft/resourcepacks/ and turn it on under Options → Resource Packs. On a server every player needs it, so offer it as the server resource pack.
  2. Put the data pack ZIP in <world>/datapacks/ (on a server, next to level.dat).
  3. Leave the world and open it again, or restart the server. /reload is not enough: the game reads the list of paintings only when the world loads.
  4. Use a give command, or hang a plain painting on a wall big enough for yours.

Version notes

New paintings exist from 1.21; title and author show from 1.21.2. The give command uses entity_data up to 1.21.4 and painting/variant from 1.21.5. On 1.20 – 1.20.6 only vanilla paintings can be replaced. We checked all of this on vanilla servers from 1.20.4 to 26.3.

How to use the generator

Add a picture, set its size in blocks, adjust the look, and download.

New or replaced

A new painting hangs next to the vanilla ones and leaves them all in the game. It needs the resource pack and the data pack, on 1.21 or newer. Replacing swaps the picture of a vanilla painting and keeps its size; only the resource pack is needed, so it also works on 1.20 and on servers you do not run.

Size and crop

Paintings are measured in blocks, from 1 to 16 each way. Crop to fill keeps the frame full and lets you slide the cut across the picture; whole image adds a border in a color you choose. Match the image shape sets the blocks from the picture's proportions.

Pixels and colors

Vanilla paintings use 16 pixels per block, so at 16 a photo turns into chunky pixel art that sits well among them. 32 or 64 keep faces and lettering readable. Fewer colors with dithering give a flat, painted look, and a little extra contrast helps in dim rooms.

Getting it on a wall

The give command hands you that exact painting. A plain painting picks at random from the placeable list, among the paintings that fit the free wall; tick the option above to make yours the only candidates.

When it does not work

Purple and black squares

The game knows the painting but not its picture: the resource pack is missing or turned off for that player, or it was built with a different namespace than the data pack.

Failed to get element

The data pack is not loaded yet. After adding it, reopen the world or restart the server. /reload lists the pack but does not add its paintings, and until the restart the random painting list fails to load.

The world will not load

A painting wider or taller than 16 blocks makes the game refuse every data pack, and the world does not open. The generator never writes more than 16; look for a file edited by hand.

Versions

Java Edition 1.20 to 26.3. Paintings became data-driven in 1.21, title and author arrived in 1.21.2, the painting item got its own painting/variant component in 1.21.5, and 1.21.9 moved pack.mcmeta to min_format and max_format. The generator writes whatever the version you pick expects. Bedrock Edition is not supported.
